What we need from you

One Decision, Four Questions

Not whether the analysis is sound. Whether these eleven problems are the ones Incubeta should be known for, in this order.

  1. Is anything here something we should not be selling?A problem can be real, common and profitable and still not be us.
  2. Is anything missing that we are known for, or want to be?We name the gaps we already know about. We are more interested in the ones we have not seen.
  3. Does the order reflect where we actually want to lead?The ranking comes from a scoring method, not from strategy. If the two disagree, strategy should win. Turn on re-score mode and move it yourself.
  4. Is there anything here we cannot credibly deliver today?Selling a problem we cannot resolve is worse than not selling it.

Worth arguing about

Three Tensions, Not Three Conclusions

The findings most likely to divide the room. Each ends in a question back to you.

The most universal problem we solve is one of the least ownable

Every client has problem 2. Not one of them is short of agencies promising to prove marketing works. Frequency gets us the meeting. Ownability wins the account. Those two point at different problems, and the sharpest things we own are 1, 3, 6 and 8, none of which is what a client raises first.

Do we want to be known for the problem everyone has, or the problems only we can see?

Two of our eleven have no voice in our own published POV

Problems 3 and 7 are not in the 2027 Marketing Outlook. Problem 3 ties for third here and gets a passing mention. Problem 7 gets nothing: our Outlook has no retention chapter at all. The Bow Tie Funnel is our own IP, and a reader would not know we think about the customer past the close.

Is that a gap in what we publish, or a signal that we do not really sell these?

One of our best compounding sales can harm the client

Problem 3 leads naturally into selling Creative at Scale. A client who also has problem 8, where output has converged and the brand has stopped being distinctive, does not need more production capacity. Selling it to them is selling a faster route to being forgettable.

Does our commercial model let a seller say no to the easy second sale?

How we built this, and how much to trust it

Where the list came from. Eleven frameworks, the execution sequence and the Incubeta value pyramid, applied to one question: what does a client actually have, that we can see before they let us in, and that resolves to something we sell.

The three tests a problem had to pass. It costs the client something real and named. We can see it from the outside before we have access to their data, which is what makes it a way in rather than a delivery task. It resolves to a specific thing we sell, not a general capability.

How much to trust the order: less than it looks. Because two axes are weighted double, one judgment call moves a problem several places. Problem 2 swings five places on a single internal opinion. The order is a disciplined starting point, not a finding.

What has been checked. Built from the frameworks, then checked against the 2027 Marketing Outlook, which added three problems we had missed entirely and reframed a fourth. Both passes were then reviewed by an independent critic that reversed one of our conclusions.

What has not been checked, and it is the big one. Both lenses are internal. Framework logic and our own published POV agree with each other more than either agrees with reality. Nothing here reflects what clients have actually paid for. The fix is a weekend of work: classify the last eight to twelve signed scopes against these eleven and see which carry revenue.

Our prediction, recorded now so it can be wrong. Problem 11 will carry more signed revenue than its last-place ranking deserves, because it is the easiest thing to raise a purchase order against. If that holds, the conversation changes from "these are our problems" to "here is the gap between what we enter on and what we get paid for."

The three Outcome Cards

All three are templates. They carry unmeasured baselines, unfilled targets and unnamed owners because no account is attached, so all three read BLOCKED. That is the gate working. Every failing field closes at the first workshop.

Staked · Problem 1

OC-01 Cited in the answer, and it moves someone

Outcome
The customer researching in an answer engine encounters the brand as a cited source inside the synthesized answer and enters the consideration set with the brand already present, instead of receiving a competitor-sourced answer.
Behavior change
Problem Aware to Solution Aware, with the brand in the initial consideration set rather than met later inside a competitor-framed comparison.
Owner required
Owns web content governance and can commit development capacity. This field kills this outcome more often than any other, because content and dev sit in different organizations.
Evidence
Fixed-interval prompt-set citation audit, same prompts and engines, logged, paired with referral-path and stage-transition data.
Kill criteria
If citation share rises and qualified referral does not move after a full quarter of stable citation, the thesis is wrong and we say so. Citation count alone is a vanity metric.
Anti-unbundling
The live risk is this being bought as an SEO line item priced per page. Per-page pricing caps the value at the page count and hands procurement a lever at every renewal.
Staked · Problem 3

OC-02 The brand's why lands in the buyer's language

Outcome
The actively comparing customer moves to preferring the brand, because the creative now carries the brand's why expressed in the terms the buyer's why is actually held in.
Behavior change
Solution Aware to Brand Aware. We are not creating demand, we are winning a comparison that is already running.
Owner required
The brand lead who owns creative approval and the performance lead who owns the media carrying it. If those are two people, one is named and the other agrees to it out loud.
Evidence
Pre and post brand-preference study against a holdout, paired with creative-level stage-transition data.
Kill criteria
If the flight beats control on engagement and moves neither brand preference nor consideration-set inclusion, the thesis is wrong for that segment. Engagement lift alone is the vanity result, and a seductive one.
Watch
Gate the Creative at Scale follow-on until problem 8 is resolved.
Standing third · Problem 2

OC-03 Budget follows the moments that change behavior

Outcome
The customer converts at a higher rate at the lifecycle stage the contribution model proved causal, because budget moved there from the stage platform attribution over-credits.
The trap
"The CFO trusts the numbers and budget gets approved" is not this outcome. That is an internal efficiency. Proof is the instrument; the outcome is the reallocation it licenses and the customer behavior that buys. If a draft has no customer in it, it is not this card.
Owner required
The VP or Director who owns the planning cycle and can actually move budget. Not the analytics lead, who can build a flawless model and change nothing.
Evidence
Geo holdout or matched-market incrementality test, with stage-transition rate moving ahead of revenue.
Kill criteria
If the holdout shows no significant difference at the end of the test, the thesis is wrong for that client and we stop. We do not extend the test until it gives us the answer we wanted. Stated before the test runs, not after.
Stake it when
The client needs the choice between a visibility bet and a proof bet.
What we should stop saying
  • "We solve agency fragmentation." That is our problem framed as theirs. No CMO has it on their scorecard.
  • "We make you faster and cheaper with AI." Take the AI out of the sentence and nothing is left. Our own Outlook says it plainly: speed is not effectiveness, and automation can extend a strong idea or multiply a weak one.
  • "We help you produce more content." Problem 8 is a published argument against our own most-requested capability.
  • "We are an AI agency." Retired. We are a marketing outcomes agency, and AI is the engine.
  • Any problem that is a capability with a question mark in front of it. "Do you have a creative scale problem?" is a pitch, not a diagnosis.

One small thing to settle. Our Outlook says "a marketing outcomes agency, powered by AI." Our canon says "a marketing outcomes agency, and AI is the engine." Both are fine. Pick one so the room does not hear two.

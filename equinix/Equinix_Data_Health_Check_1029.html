Paid Media Lead Attribution Issues

In scope · #4Ad-hoc request · #5.1$$
Equinix askedWe identified an issue where leads originating from Paid Media are being attributed to Organic. When users land on the website through a paid campaign and navigate to another page before submitting a form, UTMs and GCLIDs are lost. As a result, these Paid Media attribution values (UTMs and GCLIDs) are not passed into Salesforce, causing the lead to be incorrectly attributed. That is why we would like to discuss what other potential attribution or tracking issues Incubeta can help us identify and address beyond this known issue.

How We'd Address It

The fix for the known issue: capture the campaign parameters (UTMs and GCLID) when a visitor lands, store them in a first-party cookie that respects consent, and write them into hidden fields on every form so they reach Salesforce whichever page the form is on. Keeping both first-touch and latest-touch values also gives attribution a cleaner input.

We have sized it: of 1,775 recent leads Salesforce calls organic or blank that we could match to GA4, 210 (12%) arrived on a paid click, and 207 of those submitted the form on a different page. This is a floor: only 42% of leads can be matched today.

Beyond the known issue, we would check:

  • Handoffs between domains and form hosts that drop the session.
  • Self-referrals that overwrite the original source.
  • Consent-mode settings and their effect on paid sessions.
  • Redirects that drop parameters.
  • Whether qualified leads are sent back to Google Ads as offline conversions.

Result: paid leads credited to paid media in Salesforce, and a list of any other tracking gaps with a fix for each.

SOW Fit

Getting GCLIDs into Salesforce supports offline conversion imports and value-based bidding, which are in scope under conversion architecture (#4). The wider tracking check is an ad-hoc request (#5.1). Changes to the Salesforce attribution data layer itself need a separate SOW (#4.1). Website and form changes are made by your web team, to our specification.

Next Steps

  1. IncubetaWrite the capture specification.
  2. EquinixYour web team implements the hidden-field capture; Incubeta confirms GCLIDs reach Salesforce and the offline conversion imports.
  3. EquinixApprove the wider tracking check as an ad-hoc request, if wanted.

Why it matters: a daily check that the MAL-to-MQL ratio stays within its normal range would have flagged it on the first day. Where checks exist, the two pipeline breaks in the last 120 days were caught within a day. Breaks in the unchecked queries behind this report went unnoticed for 24 to 58 days.

We would add automated daily checks on the tables the report reads:

  • Freshness: each source arrived on time.
  • Volume: MALs, MQLs, SALs, SQLs and Opportunities stay within their trailing 28-day range.
  • Logic: stages stay in order, for example SQLs never exceed SALs for the same cohort.
  • Reconciliation: report totals match Salesforce counts.

Any failed check alerts your QA resource the same day. This health check, rerun weekly, becomes the shared status view.

Result: issues caught within a day instead of weeks.

F1

Most BigQuery compute reads one unpartitioned table

Separate SOW · #4.1$$
Problem
85% of 90-day compute reads GA4_Details. Usage of the reporting table grew, and because it isn't partitioned, every query scans all 209 million rows. Partitioning makes every query cheaper.
Solution
Build a partitioned copy (by Event_Date, clustered on common filters), dry-run sampled reports against it, then cut over.
Outcome
An estimated ~85% scan cut on the two main report sets: about $24.8K every 90 days, ~$100K a year at list price. To validate with a dry run first; we stop if the cut is under 50%.
SOW fit
Rebuilding GA4_Details as a partitioned table is a pipeline rebuild, so it needs a separate SOW (#4.1). The saving lands on your GCP bill (#8.4).

Equinix owns first-line data stewardship of reports and dashboards. The division below is the agreed operating boundary; work on the Equinix side of the line that Equinix asks RocketSource to perform is handled as an ad-hoc request under Section 5.1.

RocketSource (within the committed allocation)Equinix internal resource
  • Campaign-affecting changes and their verification at deployment (bids, budgets, conversion actions).
  • Repair of ad-platform pipeline breakages that block the standing reporting.
  • Accuracy of the standing CPL, CPO, CPW, and CAC reporting as delivered.
  • First-line data validation and routine QA of reports and dashboards, including reports built on the attribution data layer.
  • First-line investigation of data discrepancies in reports before escalation to RocketSource.
  • Routine maintenance monitoring of the ad-platform data sources (scheduled query health, source-file arrivals).
  • Monitoring, quality assertions, exception remediation, and recalibration of the attribution data layer. Any such work Equinix asks RocketSource to perform requires a separate SOW (Section 4.1).

Equinix shall designate the internal resource responsible for the right-hand column at execution and maintain that coverage for the term.

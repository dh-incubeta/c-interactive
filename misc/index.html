AI-Ready Core Thesis

Most AI programmes fail on the floor beneath them, not the model above them.

The brands that win from here are the ones whose marketing is connected and compounds into an advantage competitors can’t buy. That compounding has two prerequisites — and neither of them is a model.

01

Work mapped clearly enough for AI to act on it.

Not a process document that sits in a drive. A machine-legible map of who does what, in what order, with what inputs, and where the decision actually gets made.

02

Knowledge structured well enough for AI to use it.

Assets that are findable, current, tagged and governed. An agent given an unmanaged shared drive produces confident, well-formatted, unusable output.

Architecture

From scattered files to an agent-ready source of truth.

Four layers, built in order. Each one is worthless without the one below it — which is why the sequence matters more than the speed.

01

Inventory and validation

What exists, what is current, what is trustworthy.

Before anything is tagged or governed, we establish ground truth. Most enterprise marketing organisations discover here that a meaningful share of their live-facing content is orphaned, duplicated, or out of date — and that no single person can say which version is authoritative.

 ▾
02

Metadata and taxonomy

Every asset tagged for team, channel, audience, lifecycle status, owner.

A shared vocabulary is what turns a folder into a queryable system. This is the layer that decides whether a future agent can answer “what approved creative do we have for this audience in this market” — or whether it has to guess.

 ▾
03

Governance — maker-checker

A human approval gate between ‘a document exists’ and ‘a document is trusted.’

The control that makes the rest defensible in a regulated environment. Nothing enters the trusted layer without a named human accepting it. This is the same principle your business already applies to material that reaches a client — extended to the material that will train and feed your systems.

 ▾
04

Knowledge graph

Shared entities and relationships connecting business content with structured data.

The point at which analytics and AI see one business rather than two. Campaign performance, audience definitions, creative assets and product taxonomy resolve to the same entities — so a question asked of the data and a question asked of the content return consistent answers.

 ▾

Methodology

Not documentation. A decision framework.

Five stages. The output isn’t a process map on a wall — it’s a defensible position on which decisions a machine may make, which it may assist, and which stay with your people.
